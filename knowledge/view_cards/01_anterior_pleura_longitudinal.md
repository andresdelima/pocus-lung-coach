## Purpose
This is the foundation view of every lung exam: confirm you are looking at the pleura before judging anything else [L08 p3][M21 p4–5][N25 p2]. It is also the first view used to check lung sliding [L08 p7].

## Setup
- **Patient:** supine or semi-recumbent [L08 p3]. Sitting upright or lateral decubitus also work [PC23 §Patient Preparation].
- **Machine:** lung or abdominal preset [PC23 §Ultrasound Machine Preparation]. If you're right-handed, put the machine on the patient's right side, so one hand holds the probe and the other works the controls [PC23 §Ultrasound Machine Preparation].
- **Probe:**
  - Linear (about 5–13 MHz) for pleural detail [N25 p1–2][M21 p4].
  - A microconvex or convex probe also works; the BLUE protocol used a 5 MHz microconvex [L08 p3]. Any probe is acceptable [M21 p1], but lung sliding is easiest to see with the linear probe because the pleura is shallow [PC23 §Identify Lung Sliding].
- **Placement:** anterior chest, upper zone (between the sternum and the anterior axillary line) [M21 p4]. In the 6-point exam this is point R1/L1: the **midclavicular line, 2nd intercostal space**, on each side [PC23 §Step 1].
- **Anchor:** rest the probe firmly in the space between two ribs [PC23 §Step 1].
- **Orientation:** probe perpendicular to the ribs, crossing two neighboring ribs, with the marker toward the head (cephalad) [B24 p10][M21 p5]. Marker toward the head is the convention on every longitudinal view (author).

## Target image (must see)
1. **Two rib shadows:** two rib surfaces with acoustic shadows beneath them [L08 p3][B24 p1].
2. **Pleural line:** the pleural line between them, about 0.5 cm deeper than the rib line, with about 2 cm visible between the ribs (adult) [L08 p3]. Rib shadows plus the pleural line make the **bat sign** [L08 p3].

## What to judge (not graded as landmarks)
- In a clip: the pleural line shimmers with breathing (lung sliding) [L08 p3][B24 p10]. Sliding can't be judged on a still.
- The artifact pattern below the pleura: A-lines (horizontal repeats of the pleural line at a spacing equal to the skin-to-pleura distance, numbered A1, A2, A3 going deeper) or B-lines [L08 p3][B24 p2][PC23 §Identify A-lines].

## Recommended settings
- **Depth:** start at about 4–6 cm with the linear probe *(author-approved starting value)*, pleural line in the upper half of the screen, ideally about one-third down the screen [N25 p2], and deep enough to see one or two A-lines below it.
- **Focus:** at the pleural line [N25 p2].
- **Gain:** raise it until the pleural line is crisp, then stop before noise fills the area under it [N25 p2].
- **Modes:** avoid modes that suppress artifacts, because the lung exam reads artifacts [L08 p8].

## Common errors and fixes (Probe Guidance)
| What you see | Likely cause | Coach says |
|---|---|---|
| One long bright line, no rib shadows | Probe parallel to the ribs (lying in the interspace) or on the sternum | "Rotate 90° so the probe crosses two ribs; find both rib shadows." [M21 p4–5] |
| Only one rib shadow | Probe slid over a rib | "Slide a little up or down until a rib sits at each edge of the screen." |
| Pleural line slanted or blurred | Probe not perpendicular to the chest wall | "Tilt the probe back to 90° to the skin until the pleural line is flat and bright." |
| Pleural line in the lower half of the screen | Depth too deep | "Reduce depth until the pleural line is about a third of the way down." [N25 p2] |
| Pleural line hard to separate from noise | Gain too high | "Lower gain until the area below the pleura is dark apart from the artifacts." [N25 p2] |
| Can't tell if you're on pleura (emphysema, obesity) | Hard window | "Stay longitudinal and confirm the bat sign before going on." [M21 p5] |
| Pleural line visible but no A-lines | Beam not perpendicular to the pleura | "Rock the probe until it's at a right angle to the pleural line. The A-lines will appear." [PC23 §Identify A-lines] |
| Sliding hard to judge on a curved or phased-array probe | Low-frequency probe on a shallow target | "Switch to the linear probe; sliding is easiest to see with it. Or use M-mode." [PC23 §Identify Lung Sliding] |

## Findings to teach (Findings Tutor: educational only)
- **Normal:** lung sliding plus A-lines [N25 p2]. One or two isolated B-lines can be normal [L08 p3][B24 p9].
- **Why A-lines appear:** healthy, air-filled lung scatters the beam, so the lung tissue itself is invisible. What you see are reverberations of the pleural line [PC23 §Lung Ultrasound Anatomy][L08 p3].
- **A-lines don't prove the lung is normal:** they're also present in pneumothorax, because the parietal pleura still reflects the beam [PC23 §Identify A-lines][L08 p3].
- **Sliding** is the visceral pleura gliding on the parietal pleura with breathing. It looks like a fine shimmer travelling along the line [PC23 §Identify Lung Sliding][B24 p10].
- **Sliding seen** means the two pleural layers are touching at this spot, and only at this spot. Check the other points of the exam too [N25 p2][B24 p4][PC23 §Pneumothorax].
- **Sliding not seen** is a finding to report, not a conclusion: it has several possible causes [L08 p3][B24 p6]. Next image to get: look for a lung pulse or a lung point (card 03) [L08 p3][B24 p4–5].

## Quality checklist (Quality Grader)
- Landmarks: both rib shadows and the pleural line (bat sign).
- Orientation: longitudinal across two ribs, marker toward the head, pleural line horizontal.
- Depth: pleural line in the upper half of the screen (ideal: about one-third down). Score 2 anywhere in the upper half; 1 only if it sits in the lower half or there is clearly wasted depth *(author calibration, 2026-11-23)*.
- Gain/focus: pleural line crisp, focus at the pleura.
- Interpretability: sliding (clip only) and the A- vs B-line pattern can be judged.

## The 6-point exam (where this view fits)
Three points per side [PC23 §Defining the 6-Point Lung Ultrasound Exam]:
- **R1/L1, anterior:** midclavicular line, 2nd intercostal space (this card) [PC23 §Step 1].
- **R2/L2, lateral:** midaxillary line, around the 6th–7th intercostal space (card 02) [PC23 §Step 2].
- **R3/L3, posterolateral (PLAPS point):** posterior axillary line, between ribs 10 and 12 (card 04) [PC23 §Step 3].

At each point, check three things in order: bat sign, then lung sliding, then A-lines or B-lines [PC23 §Step 1][PC23 §Step 2].

## Next view
