## Purpose
Show the widest stretch of pleura to judge A-lines vs B-lines and score aeration at each point of the exam [M21 p4–5].

## Setup
- **Starting point:** from card 01, rotate the probe about 90° so it lies along the intercostal space, between two ribs [M21 p4–5].
- **Orientation convention (author):** after the 90° clockwise turn, the probe marker points toward the patient's right.
- **Exam: 6 points, three per side** [PC23 §Defining the 6-Point Lung Ultrasound Exam]:
  - **R1/L1, anterior:** midclavicular line, 2nd intercostal space [PC23 §Step 1].
  - **R2/L2, lateral:** midaxillary line, about the 6th–7th intercostal space [PC23 §Step 2].
  - **R3/L3, posterolateral (PLAPS point):** posterior axillary line, between ribs 10 and 12 (card 04) [PC23 §Step 3].
- **Patient:** for the lateral points, ask the patient to raise the arm on that side and rest the hand behind the head [PC23 §Patient Preparation].
- **Probe:** a linear probe for the pleura and artifacts. Switch to convex or phased array if a dense, tissue-like area appears under the pleura [M21 p4].

## Target image (must see)
1. **Continuous pleural line:** a long, continuous pleural line across the screen, with no rib shadows.
2. **Enough depth:** enough depth below the pleura to follow vertical artifacts to the bottom of the screen [L08 p3].

## What to judge (not graded as landmarks)
- In a clip: lung sliding [L08 p3][B24 p10]. Sliding can't be judged on a still; the B-line count and the aeration score can.
- The artifact pattern: A-lines, B-lines (and how many), or a tissue-like area.

## Recommended settings
- **Depth:** start at about 6–8 cm *(author-approved starting value)*. Pleural line in the upper half, ideally about one-third down, with enough depth to see whether vertical artifacts reach the bottom edge [N25 p2][L08 p3].
- **Focus:** at the pleural line [N25 p2].
- **Gain:** pleural line crisp, no noise under it. Too much gain can mimic or merge B-lines; too little hides them [N25 p2].

## Common errors and fixes
| What you see | Coach says |
|---|---|
| Rib shadow cutting through the image | "Rotate slightly so the probe lies fully inside the interspace." |
| Short pleural segment | "Rotate further along the rib line to widen the pleura." [M21 p4–5] |
| Short vertical lines that fade before the bottom, A-lines still visible | "Those look like Z-lines, not B-lines. Real B-lines reach the bottom of the screen and erase the A-lines. Check depth and gain before counting." [L08 p3][AL22 §Normal Lung Findings on POCUS] |
| Dense, tissue-like area under the pleura with a linear probe | "Switch to a convex or phased-array probe to see it in depth." [M21 p4] |
| Bandage, drain or air under the skin blocks the view | "Mark this point as not assessable rather than guessing." [M21 p11–12] |

## Findings to teach (educational only)
- **B-line definition:** a bright, sharply defined vertical artifact. It starts at the pleural line, runs all the way to the bottom of the image without fading out, wipes out the A-lines it crosses, and moves with sliding [L08 p3][N25 p2].
- **B-lines vs Z-lines:** other vertical "comet-tail" artifacts exist, but none has all the B-line features [L08 p3]. Z-lines are short vertical lines that fade out before the bottom of the screen [AL22 §Normal Lung Findings on POCUS]. Quick check:
  - **Reaches the bottom of the screen?** B-line yes, Z-line no.
  - **A-lines erased where it crosses?** B-line yes, Z-line no.
  - **Bright and sharp all the way down?** B-line yes; a Z-line is short and fades.
  - Depth too shallow can make any vertical line look like it "reaches the bottom". Keep enough depth before counting [L08 p3].
- **Counting:** 3 or more B-lines in one view is a positive (B+) point; 1–2 can be normal [L08 p3][M21 p1–2]. Beyond 3–4 B-lines, estimate the percent of the screen they fill and divide by 10 (70% ≈ 7 B-lines) [M21 p10].
- **Aeration score per point (0–3)** [M21 p4][N25 p2]:
  - **0:** A-lines, or 2 or fewer B-lines.
  - **1:** 3 or more well-spaced B-lines.
  - **2:** coalescent B-lines. In the modified score: B-lines or small subpleural consolidations covering clearly 50% or more of the pleura.
  - **3:** tissue-like pattern (consolidation).
- **Hard cases:** the score-1 vs score-2 call (whether B-lines cover 50% of the pleura) is where both humans and AI disagree most. Say so when unsure [N25 p6].

## Quality checklist
- Landmarks: continuous pleural line without rib shadows.
- Orientation: probe along the interspace, perpendicular to the skin, marker to the patient's right.
- Depth: pleura in the upper half (ideal: about one-third), artifacts traceable to the bottom.
- Gain/focus: no merged noise, focus at the pleura.
- Interpretability: B-lines told apart from Z-lines, and an aeration score of 0–3 can be assigned.
