## Purpose
Look for fluid and consolidation where they collect in a supine patient: the lower posterolateral chest above the diaphragm [L08 p3–4][B24 p6].

## Setup
- **Probe:** convex/curvilinear or phased array (about 1–5 MHz) for depth. A linear probe can't reach deep enough [B24 p1, p6].
- **Placement:** posterior axillary line, low, as far back toward the bed as the patient allows. In immobile patients, use the most dependent point you can reach [B24 p6]. In the 6-point exam this is R3/L3, the **PLAPS point**: posterior axillary line, in a rib space between ribs 10 and 12. In a supine patient, slide the probe under the patient's side to get there [PC23 §Step 3].
- **Patient:** ask the patient to raise the arm on that side and rest the hand behind the head [PC23 §Patient Preparation].
- **Orientation:** longitudinal, marker toward the head (cephalad), same convention as the other cards [PC23 §Step 3].
- **Landmarks first:** liver (right) or spleen (left), then the moving bright diaphragm above it [B24 p6]. The kidney and the spine are often visible too. In a normal scan the spine stops at the diaphragm and does not continue up into the chest [PC23 §Step 3].

## Target image (must see)
1. **Liver or spleen:** at the bottom of the image.
2. **Diaphragm:** the diaphragm as a curved bright line moving with breathing [B24 p6, p8].
3. **Space above the diaphragm:** the space just above the diaphragm (the chest side), where fluid would collect [B24 p6].
4. **Lung tissue:** lung tissue above the diaphragm: aerated lung (curtain or mirror image), or consolidated lung, or lung within fluid [B24 p3–4][PC23 §Mirror Image Artifact].
5. **Chest wall:** at the top.

## Recommended settings
- **Depth:** start at about 12–16 cm with the curvilinear probe *(author-approved starting value)*, enough to show the liver or spleen, the diaphragm and, if there is fluid, the spine behind it.
- **Mode:** B-mode first. M-mode across any fluid shows the sinusoid sign [L08 p3–4][B24 p6].

## Common errors and fixes
| What you see | Coach says |
|---|---|
| Only aerated lung, no organs | "Slide down one or two rib spaces toward the costophrenic angle until the liver or spleen appears." [B24 p4] |
| Only liver or spleen, no diaphragm | "Slide up and fan slightly toward the head to bring the diaphragm into view." |
| Left side hidden by stomach gas | "Move more posterior along the ribs and use the spleen as your window." [B24 p8] |
| Rib shadows block the fluid | "Rotate slightly to line up with the rib space." |
| Linear probe, deep structures not seen | "Switch to a curvilinear or phased-array probe." [B24 p6] |

## Findings to teach (educational only)
Teach these as image patterns to recognize. Report what is seen; don't name a cause.
- **Normal curtain sign:** with each breath, aerated lung slides down over the diaphragm and organs and hides them; they reappear on expiration [B24 p4, p10][PC23 §Curtain Sign]. Losing it is a finding to report [B24 p4].
- **Mirror image (normal):** above the diaphragm, aerated lung shows a mirror copy of the liver or spleen [PC23 §Mirror Image Artifact].
- **Fluid above the diaphragm:**
  - An anechoic (black) or hypoechoic space above the diaphragm [B24 p6].
  - **Spine sign:** the spine stays visible above the diaphragm, because fluid carries sound [B24 p6][PC23 §Spine Sign].
  - **Quad sign:** fluid boxed in by the pleural line, the rib shadows and the lung line [L08 p3].
  - **Sinusoid sign (M-mode):** the lung line moves toward the chest wall with each breath [L08 p3–4].
  - **What the fluid looks like:** plain black, or with strands (septations), debris or swirling particles (the plankton sign) [B24 p6–7]. Fluid split into separate pockets is called loculated [PC23 §Loculated Pleural Effusion]. Heavier material layering at the bottom is the hematocrit sign [PC23 §Hematocrit Sign][B24 p7].
  - **Jellyfish sign:** compressed lung floating in a large collection of fluid [B24 p10][PC23 §Jellyfish Sign].
- **Tissue-like lung (consolidation):**
  - Lung that looks like liver (hepatization) [B24 p3].
  - A ragged deep border with aerated lung (the **shred sign**) [L08 p4][B24 p3].
  - **Air bronchograms:** bright dots or branches inside it, either moving with breathing (dynamic) or not moving (static) [B24 p3][N25 p3]. Describe which you see; telling the causes apart needs more than ultrasound [PC23 §Pneumonia][N25 p3].

## Quality checklist
- Landmarks: liver or spleen, diaphragm, the space above the diaphragm, lung tissue, chest wall.
- Orientation: longitudinal, marker toward the head, diaphragm across the image.
- Depth: whole diaphragm shown, plus the spine if fluid is present.
- Gain/focus: fluid looks black (not gray), organ texture visible.
- Interpretability: can call fluid above the diaphragm yes or no, and tissue-like lung yes or no, as image findings. Clip or M-mode for motion signs.
